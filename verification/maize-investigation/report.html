1. Research objective

Research Objective: Discovering Drought-Resilient Maize Genotypes in West Africa

Background

Climate change and increasingly unpredictable rainfall threaten maize production across sub-Saharan Africa. Developing drought-resilient maize varieties is essential for improving agricultural productivity and food security.

I want to investigate whether the available experimental datasets contain evidence that certain maize genotypes consistently perform better under drought stress.

Primary Research Question

Which maize genotypes demonstrate the strongest drought tolerance while maintaining relatively stable grain yield, and which measurable plant characteristics are most strongly associated with that resilience?

Instructions to NeuroLab AI

Act as an autonomous scientific research agent.

Use the available maize research datasets, including the University of Ghana/WACCI drought-tolerance dataset and relevant IITA experimental data where scientifically appropriate.

Conduct the investigation through the following stages.

Stage 1 — Dataset Exploration

1. Identify all relevant available datasets.
2. Examine their structures, variables, experimental conditions, and provenance.
3. Identify drought-stressed and well-watered observations.
4. Examine missing values and potential data-quality problems.
5. Determine whether observations can be compared or combined without compromising scientific validity.

Do not combine unrelated experiments or genotype identifiers without a defensible scientific basis.

Stage 2 — Hypothesis Generation

Generate at least three scientifically testable hypotheses.

Consider hypotheses involving:

* Grain yield stability under drought stress.
* Anthesis–silking interval and drought resilience.
* Stay-green characteristics and grain yield.
* Genotype-by-environment interactions.

Explain the scientific rationale for each hypothesis.

Stage 3 — Experimental Design

Design an appropriate statistical investigation.

Specify:

* Independent and dependent variables.
* Experimental groups and environmental conditions.
* Statistical methods.
* Confounding factors.
* Assumptions and limitations.
* Criteria for evaluating each hypothesis.

Prioritize methods appropriate for the actual experimental design and available observations.

Stage 4 — Autonomous Experimentation

Execute the analyses using the platform’s available scientific tools.

Calculate, where supported:

1. Mean grain yield under drought stress.
2. Mean grain yield under well-watered conditions.
3. Relative yield retention.
4. Yield variability across environments.
5. Trait correlations.
6. Genotype performance rankings.
7. Relevant statistical uncertainty.

Do not invent observations or statistical results.

Distinguish observed data, calculated statistics, model predictions, and AI interpretations.

Stage 5 — Evidence Evaluation

Evaluate each hypothesis against the actual experimental evidence.

For every hypothesis:

* Present the relevant results.
* Explain whether the evidence supports it.
* Report uncertainty and limitations.
* Identify contradictory findings.
* Explain what cannot be concluded from the available data.

Avoid treating correlations as proof of causation.

Stage 6 — Autonomous Scientific Reasoning

Based on the results:

1. Identify the most promising genotype candidates.
2. Explain why they deserve further investigation.
3. Identify weaknesses in the available evidence.
4. Propose the next scientifically useful experiment.
5. Explain what additional data would improve confidence.

Where supported, autonomously select and execute one follow-up analysis that could reduce an important uncertainty.

Stage 7 — Scientific Visualization

Generate meaningful visualizations, including:

* Genotype yield comparisons.
* Drought versus well-watered performance.
* Trait correlation heatmaps.
* Yield stability across experimental environments.
* Evidence supporting the leading genotype candidates.

Use actual experimental results.

Stage 8 — Final Research Report

Produce a structured scientific report containing:

1. Research objective.
2. Dataset sources and provenance.
3. Hypotheses.
4. Methodology.
5. Statistical findings.
6. Visualizations.
7. Most promising genotype candidates.
8. Scientific limitations.
9. Recommended next experiments.
10. Reproducibility information.

Include the actual tools, parameters, datasets, and computational steps used.

Autonomous Execution Requirements

Execute the investigation from beginning to end without requiring approval between ordinary analysis stages.

Show genuine execution progress, including dataset inspection, hypothesis generation, tool execution, evidence evaluation, and report generation.

Do not simulate tool calls, scientific results, or execution progress.

If an operation is unsupported, explicitly identify the limitation and continue with a scientifically defensible alternative.

Expected Outcome

At the end of this investigation, I should understand:

* Which maize genotypes appear most drought-resilient.
* Which plant characteristics are associated with resilience.
* How strongly the available evidence supports these findings.
* What additional experiments should be performed.

        4. Methodology

        assumptions and limits
        • Dataset profile is descriptive; ENVT codes are not interpreted as calendar years.
        • STYG is structurally absent for the well-watered source regime, by design.
        • Exploratory observational contrast; water regime is confounded with environment and this is not a causal treatment effect.
        • Only three ENVT indices and two replications are represented; generalization is limited.
        • The yield-retention ratio is descriptive and is null if the well-watered denominator is not positive.
        • Descriptive exploratory ranking only; alpha-lattice effects and multiplicity are not modeled.
        • Not a field recommendation; candidates require confirmatory trials and model-based assessment.
        • Descriptive variation across observed ENVT indices, not a validated stability model or confirmatory genotype-by-environment interaction test.
        • Water regimes are summarized separately. ENVT indices are not calendar years; no environment extrapolation is justified.
        • Ranks and environmental centering use only genotypes observed in every available environment of that regime; incomplete coverage is retained and labeled.
        • Leave-one-environment-out rank changes are sensitivity analyses, not confidence intervals. Three environments cannot establish broad adaptation.
        • Alpha-lattice block effects, genotype ranking uncertainty and multiplicity are not modeled. Low variation alone does not imply high yield or drought tolerance.
        • Genotype retention joins only canonical identities within this same Ghana source, after separate regime summaries; it does not prove matched causal irrigation contrasts. A high ratio can reflect a low watered denominator.
        • Exploratory association with observed drought yield, not a causal trait effect, validated drought tolerance measure, or predictive model.
        • Each genotype contributes one equal-environment mean per trait; incomplete genotype/environment trait-yield pairs are excluded for that trait and counts/lineage are retained.
        • Percentile intervals resample genotype profiles, conditional on the observed environments; they do not quantify uncertainty across future environments or correct alpha-lattice effects.
        • Spearman ranks average ties. Constant or insufficient data produce null correlations, not zero; degenerate bootstrap draws are omitted and counted.
        • Seven exploratory trait comparisons have no multiplicity adjustment or confirmatory significance test. Stay-green score direction is not established by the source dictionary.
        • Genotype case-resampling assumes exchangeable profiles and ignores relatedness, genotype selection and within-genotype plot error; these intervals do not establish validated population coverage.

                      8. Scientific limitations

                      • Replay planner is deterministic and is not Meta inference.
                      • Ghana water regime is confounded with trial environment.
                      • Only three ENVT indices are present; do not reinterpret them as calendar years.
                      • Dataset profile is descriptive; ENVT codes are not interpreted as calendar years.
                      • STYG is structurally absent for the well-watered source regime, by design.
                      • Exploratory observational contrast; water regime is confounded with environment and this is not a causal treatment effect.
                      • Only three ENVT indices and two replications are represented; generalization is limited.
                      • The yield-retention ratio is descriptive and is null if the well-watered denominator is not positive.
                      • Descriptive exploratory ranking only; alpha-lattice effects and multiplicity are not modeled.
                      • Not a field recommendation; candidates require confirmatory trials and model-based assessment.
                      • Descriptive variation across observed ENVT indices, not a validated stability model or confirmatory genotype-by-environment interaction test.
                      • Water regimes are summarized separately. ENVT indices are not calendar years; no environment extrapolation is justified.
                      • Ranks and environmental centering use only genotypes observed in every available environment of that regime; incomplete coverage is retained and labeled.
                      • Leave-one-environment-out rank changes are sensitivity analyses, not confidence intervals. Three environments cannot establish broad adaptation.
                      • Alpha-lattice block effects, genotype ranking uncertainty and multiplicity are not modeled. Low variation alone does not imply high yield or drought tolerance.
                      • Genotype retention joins only canonical identities within this same Ghana source, after separate regime summaries; it does not prove matched causal irrigation contrasts. A high ratio can reflect a low watered denominator.
                      • Exploratory association with observed drought yield, not a causal trait effect, validated drought tolerance measure, or predictive model.
                      • Each genotype contributes one equal-environment mean per trait; incomplete genotype/environment trait-yield pairs are excluded for that trait and counts/lineage are retained.
                      • Percentile intervals resample genotype profiles, conditional on the observed environments; they do not quantify uncertainty across future environments or correct alpha-lattice effects.
                      • Spearman ranks average ties. Constant or insufficient data produce null correlations, not zero; degenerate bootstrap draws are omitted and counted.
                      • Seven exploratory trait comparisons have no multiplicity adjustment or confirmatory significance test. Stay-green score direction is not established by the source dictionary.
                      • Genotype case-resampling assumes exchangeable profiles and ignores relatedness, genotype selection and within-genotype plot error; these intervals do not establish validated population coverage.
                      • No cross-source genotype merge or matched IITA inference is justified by the supplied identifiers alone.
                      • No causal irrigation effect, calibrated crop simulation, field recommendation or validated broad adaptation is established.
                      • Template criteria are retrospective operational definitions, not preregistered tests; resampling intervals are conditional and unadjusted for multiple traits/relatedness.
